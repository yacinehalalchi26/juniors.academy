CSCA exam in Algeria: guide and free practice tests

Everything Algerian students need to know about the China Scholastic Competency Assessment, and how to practise for it online, for free.

What is the CSCA?

The China Scholastic Competency Assessment (CSCA) is the academic entrance test used by many Chinese universities to admit international students to bachelor's programmes. It's taken by students from Algeria, the rest of Africa, Asia and beyond who apply to study in China, often with a scholarship.

The exam has several papers. Mathematics is required for everyone. Physics and Chemistry depend on the programme you apply to: engineering and computer science usually ask for Physics, while medicine, pharmacy, agriculture and chemistry programmes usually ask for Chemistry. Some programmes also ask for a Chinese language paper.

Format of the science papers: 48 multiple-choice questions per module, 60 minutes per module, scored out of 100. No calculator is allowed.

Who needs the CSCA in Algeria?

  • Students who have finished the baccalauréat and are applying to a Chinese university for a bachelor's degree.
  • Applicants to Chinese Government Scholarship and university scholarship programmes that ask for a CSCA score.
  • Students changing course or university in China whose new programme asks for it.

Requirements vary between universities and change from year to year, so always check the admission page of the university you're applying to.

How to prepare from Algeria

1. Get used to the English terminology

Algerian students study science in French and Arabic, but the CSCA science papers are in English. Most of the content is familiar from the baccalauréat; the challenge is reading fast in English. Our tests use the exact vocabulary you'll meet in the exam, so the words become automatic.

2. Train your speed without a calculator

Sixty minutes for 48 questions means 75 seconds per question, with mental arithmetic only. Timed practice is the only way to build that speed.

3. Cover the topics the bac doesn't

  • Mathematics: sets and logic, functions, exponents and logarithms, trigonometry, sequences, analytic geometry, vectors, complex numbers, probability and statistics, calculus, and solid geometry.
  • Physics: mechanics, energy and momentum, circular motion and gravitation, oscillations and waves, optics, thermal physics, electricity, magnetism and induction, and modern physics.
  • Chemistry: atomic structure, bonding, moles and stoichiometry, acids and bases, redox and electrochemistry, rates and equilibrium, elements and compounds, organic chemistry, and practical chemistry.

4. Practise in the real format

Start with a 15-minute quick test to see where you stand, then take module tests for an accurate score, and a full exam of 2 or 3 modules to practise your stamina before exam day. Questions change every time, and your results come with a topic breakdown and an answer review.

Frequently asked questions

Can I take the CSCA in Algeria?

Test arrangements, including any centres or online sessions available to students in Algeria, are set by the organisers. Check the current options and dates on csca.cn.

Is a practice score from CSCA Test accepted by universities?

No. Only official CSCA results count for admission. Our scores are for your own preparation.

Which modules should I practise?

Mathematics always. Add Physics for engineering and computing programmes, Chemistry for medicine, pharmacy and life sciences, or both if you're not sure yet.
