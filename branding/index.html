The profile mark

A profile picture is seen at 32 pixels far more often than at any other size — in a comment, in search, in a message list. It is the most-viewed piece of the identity.

The prism gradient

A colour sweep runs clockwise around the circle, starting at Academy Purple at twelve o’clock and returning to it at 360°, so the seam is invisible. Eight stops, 45° apart. The direction and the order never change — a gradient that runs the other way is a different mark.

Six treatments

Use the prism ring on Instagram, TikTok and Facebook: the colour sits on the rim where the story ring goes, and the purple core keeps the monogram readable down to 32 px. Use solid purple on WhatsApp Business, the Google Business Profile, and anywhere it will be printed in one ink. Change every channel on the same day.

  • Put Sun on purple or night, as a fill or as text
  • Use Lavender and Blush to separate blocks instead of drawing more lines
  • Keep crimson to roughly one element per post
Never
  • Crimson on purple, or purple on crimson
  • Sun as text on anything light
  • A gradient between brand colours — the prism avatar is the only gradient we own
  • A new colour introduced for one campaign

06

Graphic devices

Four shapes, all taken from the logo. They are what makes a post recognisable across a room, and they are the only decoration the brand needs.

000 000 DA

The slant

A −20° skew, the angle of the J and A strokes. Price tags, bands behind headlines, photo cuts, chips, buttons.

The peak

The A without its crossbar. Bullet points, list markers, faint background patterns, dividers.

PROMO2027

The sticker

A circle rotated −12°, usually in Sun. Promo years, age badges, a price on a photo. One per post.

The rule

A 4 px line in Sun or Lavender, separating a headline from its body on explainer slides.

All four can be drawn in Canva in under a minute by someone with no design training. That is deliberate — a system nobody can execute is not a system.

Do
  • One slanted element per post, doing one job
  • The peak as a bullet, at the same size as the line of text it marks
  • One sticker, rotated −12°, in Sun
Never
  • The slant on the logo, or on body text
  • Two stickers on one post — that is a market stall
  • A decorative border round the whole canvas
  • The peak filled with a photo

The grid

Two canvases carry everything, and both are partly covered by the app they appear in. These margins are what keeps a post readable after Instagram crops the preview and lays its own buttons over the video.

CanvasSizeThe rules
Feed post1080 × 135064 px margin all round. Logo bottom-left in its own 64 px band. Headlines start below y 700 so the square preview never cuts them
Reel & story1080 × 1920Live area y 230 to 1610. The top 230 px and bottom 310 px belong to the app. The right rail takes 200 px from y 900 down

Check every feed post as a square before it goes out. That is how most people see it first, and it crops from the top and bottom. If a word or a face is lost in the square, move it.

The templates

Forty layouts, grouped by the job they do. The point is not that they look good — it is that nobody has to decide what a post should look like on the day there is something to say.

The eight used most

These cover the majority of a normal month. Build these eight properly in Canva first, with real photo crops and Poppins and Cairo loaded. Everything else can wait.

The weekly series

Ten recurring formats. A series works because it comes back on the same day with the same shape, so people learn to look for it.

Everything else

Eighteen for the situations that come up a few times a year, and four more below them for the ones that come up once or twice.

Every number, name, price and quote in these files is a placeholder. Nothing goes out carrying “000 000 DA” or an invented testimonial. Replace placeholders with real figures, publish testimonials only with permission, and apply the photography rules in section 11 to every picture of a child.
09

Carousels

A carousel is an assembly job, not a design job. Five slide roles, two orders. Pick the order, fill the slots, publish.

The cover decides everything

A photo with the title on the bottom third, never a text-only card. If it does not name the destination or ask the parent’s question, nothing after it gets seen.

One idea per slide

No slide holds two points. The step number in the corner is what keeps a thumb moving; take it off and people leave at slide three.

Alternate the texture

Photo slides are dark and full-bleed, explainer slides are pale. Switching every few slides is what stops a carousel feeling like a document.

Six to eight, then the same ending

Past eight slides the last one stops being seen — and the last one is what turns attention into a message.

The price slide is the difference. Put a real figure second to last in every carousel, in the shape of the “Chhal ?” template. Parents in this market compare quietly and decide privately. The agency that answers the price question before it is asked is the one that gets the message.

Reels

Reels are where the reach is, and almost none of the work is design. A reel cover is a frame of the video with one line of text on it. The craft is in the filming and in the first second.

Photography, and the rules that are not negotiable

Our photographs are of children. That makes this the one section where a rule is not a preference — it is a condition of publishing at all.

What our pictures look like

Daylight, real rooms, faces mid-activity rather than lined up for the camera. An adult visible in the frame wherever children are — that single compositional habit does more for a parent’s confidence than any caption. Warm, slightly underexposed rather than blown out. No heavy filters, and no colour grading that fights the palette.

Consent is not optional and not verbal. Before any photograph or video of a child is published:
  • Written permission from a parent or legal guardian, obtained before departure, stored, and renewed each trip
  • No full name, no school name, no home address, no accommodation address, no geotag on a residence
  • No image of a child distressed, unwell, undressed or asleep
  • Permission withdrawn at any time means the post comes down the same day, without discussion
  • A written register of who has consented, kept by one named person, checked before every publish
  • Other people’s children caught in the background are cropped out, or the photo is not used

Partner names and crests. A club badge, a school name, a university crest or a federation logo goes on a post only with that organisation’s written permission, and only in the form they supply. This applies to the football programmes in particular.

Stock photography: none. A stock photo of a smiling teenager on a campus is recognisable as a stock photo to exactly the parents we are trying to convince, and it undoes the work every real photograph does.

Appendix · the red-led alternative

The same system with the two brand colours swapped: crimson leads, purple acts. It is still on the table, so here is the honest case either way.

Until a decision is made, the purple system in this document is what you build. Everything else on this page applies unchanged to either route — only the two hex values swap.
Purple-ledRed-led
White on the lead colour12.5:1 — passes at every size5.0:1 — large bold text only, weak for captions and prices
How it readsConsidered, trusted, premium. Closer to how a parent wants to feelUrgent, energetic, young. Closer to how a fourteen-year-old wants to feel
In the categoryAlmost nobody uses purple. Distinctive at a glance in a feedRed is the most common colour in education and travel marketing here
PrintDeep purple is stable across papers and inksLarge flat crimson areas shift noticeably between printers
RiskCan feel formal if the photography is not warmSmall text on crimson becomes unreadable, and the “crimson = action” discipline is lost

The recommendation is purple-led. The contrast maths settles it: the lead colour sits under the most words, and white on purple is legible at every size while white on crimson is not. Keeping crimson for one action per post is also what makes that action obvious — the moment it becomes the background, nothing is urgent any more.
